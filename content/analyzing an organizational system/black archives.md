# Black Innovation Archive: Black Inventor’s Database
## Primary Resources Being Organized
- The Black Innovation Archive organizes structured historical records that are centered around the profiles of African American inventors. These profiles are linked alongside innovations, patent records, including cited primary and secondary sources. Each innovator’s entry captures what the individual engineered, patented, and/or discovered. It also includes the historical obstacles that they were faced with. The database provides, where supporting documentation is held, and an explicit evidence status (such as Verified, Documented, or Supported). 
## Primary Interactions Supported
- The Black Innovation Archive is a system that supports research-based evidence. Each piece of evidence has been thoroughly verified, and fact checked and includes exploratory browsing for users doing research. Some of the options included in the archives are: 
1.	***Browsing & Filtering:*** People browsing can look at inventor profiles and filter across time periods, geographic regions, and subject fields, or they can navigate to dedicated views like the Timeline, Map, Graph, Patents, Evidence Vault, and Education learning center.
2.	***Research & Lineage Tracing:*** Users can move from a person to an object, patent, or historical problem.
3.	***Fact-Checking & Source Evaluation:*** Profiles pair claims with cited repositories and evidence ratings, while the Learning Center guides researchers on how to inspect patent claims directly and to compare conflicting accounts. This backs the legitimacy of claims with evidence.
## Classification System and Audience Effectiveness
The Black Innovation Archive uses a faceted classification system. The archive combines both chronological events with evidential groupings. Examples include:
•	Time Periods: Ancient Africa, Medieval Africa, Early Modern, 1800s, 1900–1950, 1950–2000, and 2000–Present.
•	Geography: Regions and countries including Africa, United States, Caribbean, South America, Canada, and Europe.
•	Fields: Medicine & Health, Science & Engineering, Technology & Computing, Transportation, Agriculture, Everyday Life, Space & Aerospace, Industry & Materials, and Architecture & Infrastructure.
•	Evidence & Demographic Facets: Six evidence tiers (Verified, Documented, Supported, etc.) alongside gender and regional views (such as Women and Africa).
For students, educators, and researchers, this faceted structure is effective because innovation history crosses eras, borders, and disciplines. Filtering by both field and time Period can be time consuming and complex. Faceted is simpler and less time consuming. 
## Resource Relationships
Relationships across the archive are represented through the use of cross-linked entity records, shared facet groupings, chronological sequences, visual, and graphical views. Examples include:
1	***Inventor to invention patent Links:*** Each inventor profile connects to their specific innovations, the patent numbers, and the cited archives so that users can verify source. 
2	***Shared Facets:*** Groups records by field, region, and era. This offers researchers guided pathways when searching for inventions and builds upon how ideas spread and build upon earlier inventions. 
3	***Graph & Timeline Views:*** The site offers dedicated graph and timeline views designed to expose network and historical connections across different profiles and innovations rather than detaching biographies in individual pages.
