# Sensory Grounding

- Sensory grounding is a practical cognitive coping method that was designed to move the mind out of overwhelming anxiety, dissociation, or panic by diverting a person's attention in the immediate physical environment. When emotional distress suddenly hits, the brain suddenly becomes trapped in thoughts spiraling about past trauma and future fears. By focusing deliberately on real-time sensory, the input interrupts this stress loop by shifting brain activity away from the emotional abnormalities and back to the logical prefrontal cortex.

- The most common approach used by therapists is the ## 5-4-3-2-1 method. This method is used to guide individuals to systematically identify the following: 

 1. Five things that they can see 
 2. Four that they can physically feel 
 3. Three that they can hear 
 4. Two that they can smell
 5. One that they can taste 

- Some other variations include holding an ice cube, splashing cold water on the face, or pressing feet firmly into the floor. Sensory grounding requires no equipment and takes mere minutes. Sensory grounding offers fast, discreet stabilization that restores a sense of physical safety and mental clarity.

-  In addition, [[physical fitness]] can be looked at as a sensory item, as it assists with the touch portion of sensory grounding because you can physically touch items fitness items with your hands, legs, or feet.
