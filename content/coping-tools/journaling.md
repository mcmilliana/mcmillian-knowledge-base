# Journaling

* Journaling acts as an immediate grounding tool when a mental health crisis arises because it interrupts emotional spirals and reduces overwhelming anxiety. Journaling forces your brain to slow down to the speed of your hand, shifting brain activity from being reactive to the rational prefrontal cortex. This process, known as "affect labeling", helps you externalize internal chaotic thoughts onto paper, creating psychological distance so the feelings become observable. By putting distress into concrete words, a person gets to reduce emotional intensity and prevents rumination. Journaling can be used in addition with \[\[spirituality]] because users can write the feelings that they can't express verbally through prayer down as faith directs their messages directly to God/The creator, who is the all seeing and all hearing in most faiths.



* Additionally, journaling provides a person with a judgment-free space to be used to identify triggers, track mood swings, and anchor yourself in the present moment. This restores a sense of control. While journaling is not a replacement for professional crisis support, it serves as an easily accessible, stabilizing first-aid tool to be used to help you regain clarity and self-regulate. When a person becomes overwhelmed, they can list basic facts such as:
1. Where you are
2. What you feel
3. One small next step
